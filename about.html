About

Engineer, teacher and builder — working across civil engineering, software and materials science.

Who I am

I'm a civil engineer, STEM enthusiast, educator, and builder who enjoys exploring how things work—from materials, physics, and mathematics to software, AI, and complex systems. I like learning across disciplines and then turning what I learn into something practical, whether that's a tool, a project, a lesson, or a new idea.

A big part of what I care about is making knowledge and technology more useful and accessible. I enjoy teaching, mentoring, building open and reusable systems, and experimenting with ideas that can help people learn, create, and solve problems more effectively.

I'm still exploring where all of this leads, and that's part of the fun. I'm interested in the space where engineering, science, education, and technology meet—and in building things that have a positive, tangible impact.

Research interests

My research interests are centered on Materials Science, particularly energy materials and computational materials science. I'm especially interested in materials that can help address practical challenges in energy storage and conversion, including batteries and related technologies. I'm drawn to the intersection of materials, physics, chemistry, and computation because it offers a way to understand materials at a deeper level and design better ones.

I'm currently transitioning from my background in Civil Engineering toward Materials Science and exploring potential directions for my Master's research. My longer-term interest is in computational and experimental approaches to discovering, understanding, and optimizing materials for energy applications. I'm still refining the exact thesis topic, so I see this stage as an opportunity to explore the field broadly, build the necessary foundations, and identify a research problem where I can make a meaningful contribution.

Vision

Long term, I want to build things that genuinely move people and society forward. I'm interested in the intersection of science, engineering, education, and technology, and I want to contribute not just by working within existing systems, but by creating better ones—better ways to learn, build, solve problems, and turn ideas into reality.

I'd like to grow into someone who can move comfortably between deep technical work, research, teaching, and building real-world products and systems. Whether that leads to work in advanced materials and energy, AI and software, education, or something I haven't discovered yet, the underlying goal is the same: create useful knowledge and technology, make it more accessible, and help accelerate meaningful progress.

Ultimately, I want my work to have a positive effect beyond myself—to build things that remain useful, enable other people to do more, and contribute in some small way to a more capable and progressive society.
